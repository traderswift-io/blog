This is what makes TraderSwift different.

Most trading today runs on trust.

You trust the bot that holds your key not to lose it.

You trust a signal seller's track record.

You trust that a data feed is honest, and that a payment reached the person who earned it.

On TraderSwift, the aim is simple: where something can be checked, you check it, instead of taking anyone's word for it.

## What can you check?

**Your key.** With Signer, no single machine holds your key. Each order is checked against limits you wrote, and every yes or no is recorded on a public blockchain.

This is live today.

**Your limits.** The limits, called a policy, are kept on that blockchain. Anyone who runs a node can read them.

This is live today.

**The data.** Each charts data node publishes a daily hash of its candles. Anyone can recompute it, and compare one node against another.

This is live from our own node. Posting the hashes to the hub is being built.

**Signals.** Many people claim they offer good signals. Here, each signal is fingerprinted and timestamped before the outcome.

That part is built, off chain. Putting the fingerprints on a Signals chain is planned. Once it is live and a signal is revealed, anyone will be able to match the two.

**Agents.** An agent that trades through Signer has every order request it makes, signed or refused, recorded on a public chain.

A marketplace where those records can be found and compared is planned.

**Payments.** A builder is paid directly over x402. We never hold the money.

The design is accepted. The first real payment has not been made yet.

**The code.** Every part is open source. You can read it, or have your own AI agent read it for you.

The next chapter explains why that matters.

## Why does this matter?

A promise costs nothing. A record you can check is worth more.

When the record is public, a good agent, a good signal provider or an honest data node can prove it.

A bad one has nowhere to hide.
